# Sampler efficiency for field-level inference, and observational systematics for PNG: categorised bibliography (to 2026-10-13)

**How things were verified.** Every arXiv ID below was checked against its arxiv.org/abs page, which confirmed the title, author list and submission date (fetched 2026-10-13). The arXiv API itself returned HTTP 429 from Delta, so the abs pages were scraped one at a time. Each journal reference carries one of these tags:
- **[jref]**: taken from the arXiv journal-ref field.
- **[Crossref]**: matched by title in the Crossref API, with the DOI given.
- **[memory, unverified]**: from prior knowledge only; check before citing.

All quantitative claims are paraphrased or quoted from the abstracts or full texts linked. Claims that came only from a search-engine summary are marked as such.

**Context from the project.** Joint NUTS/MCLMC (BlackJAX) on (white noise $\varepsilon$, $f_{\rm NL}$, bias). MCLMC run at half the auto-tuned step needs about 250 gradients per effective $f_{\rm NL}$ sample at $160^3$. Andrews et al. report an $f_{\rm NL}$ correlation length of about $10^4$ Gibbs samples. Stage 4 will sample imaging-template amplitudes inside the forward model.

## A1. HMC in BORG, Gibbs blocking, and $f_{\rm NL}$ mixing in BORG

### Takeaway
BORG and LEFTfield use the same structure: HMC on the initial-condition field inside a Gibbs/block scheme, with slice sampling (or other 1-D moves) on bias, cosmology and $f_{\rm NL}$. Andrews et al. 2023 said $f_{\rm NL}$ decorrelates after about $10^3$ samples. The Euclid paper (Andrews et al. 2024/2026) instead reports about $10^4$ samples for every run where bias is sampled, while finding "little to no correlation" between $f_{\rm NL}$ and bias. The slow mixing therefore looks like a feature of the Gibbs blocking (conditional moves on a parameter strongly coupled to the field), not of a bias degeneracy.

### Inferences
- The two Andrews numbers conflict: about $10^3$ samples in 2023 versus about $10^4$ in 2024/2026, and the later paper also finds little $f_{\rm NL}$–bias correlation. The likely cause is that conditional slice moves on $f_{\rm NL}$ at fixed $\varepsilon$ are short. Under the $f_{\rm NL}\phi^2$ term, the field and $f_{\rm NL}$ are strongly coupled a posteriori, so the Gibbs move in $f_{\rm NL}$ is narrow even when the marginal $f_{\rm NL}$–bias correlation is small. Joint gradient-based moves (NUTS/MCLMC on $(\varepsilon,f_{\rm NL},b)$) avoid this, which is consistent with the project's 250 gradients/ESS. This is an inference; neither Andrews paper diagnoses the cause.
- The Andrews papers count Gibbs sweeps, not gradients. Each sweep includes one HMC trajectory of unknown leapfrog length plus slice-sampling likelihood calls, so "$10^4$ samples" cannot be converted to gradients/ESS from what I read (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Neither Euclid nor Andrews 2023 states (in the text I read) the number of leapfrog steps per HMC move or the number of likelihood evaluations per slice step. A gradients-per-ESS figure for BORG $f_{\rm NL}$ therefore cannot be derived.
- I could not confirm the A&A article number (A94) independently. The A&A URL path confirms a July 2026 A&A issue.

## A2. NUTS, microcanonical samplers (MCHMC/MCLMC/MAMS), and BlackJAX

### Takeaway
MCLMC is an energy-conserving, unadjusted sampler whose cost per effective sample stays roughly constant with dimension, whereas adjusted samplers scale roughly as $d^{1/4}$. Its asymptotic bias is controlled by the energy-error variance per dimension (EEVPD). MAMS is the Metropolis-adjusted, unbiased version and is up to about 7× faster than NUTS. All are in BlackJAX.

### Cited Findings
- **Hoffman & Gelman (2014)**, "The No-U-Turn Sampler: Adaptively Setting Path Lengths in Hamiltonian Monte Carlo", arXiv:1111.4246, JMLR 15, 1593 [memory, unverified]. Removes the need to hand-set trajectory length L via recursive tree-building and a U-turn criterion; "at least as efficiently as" well-tuned HMC. — [arXiv:1111.4246](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.4246)
- **Ver Steeg & Galstyan (2021)**, "Hamiltonian Dynamics with Non-Newtonian Momentum for Rapid Sampling", arXiv:2111.02434, NeurIPS 2021 [memory, unverified]. Energy-Sampling Hamiltonian (ESH) dynamics: deterministic dynamics that sample the target exactly under ergodicity. This is the precursor of MCHMC. — [arXiv:2111.02434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02434)
- **Robnik, De Luca, Silverstein & Seljak (2023)**, "Microcanonical Hamiltonian Monte Carlo", arXiv:2212.08549, JMLR 24 (311) [memory, unverified]. Fixed-energy dynamics; needs momentum bounces for ergodicity, and their continuous version is MCLMC. Favourable scaling with condition number and dimension; "consistently outperforms NUTS HMC ... in some cases by more than an order of magnitude". — [arXiv:2212.08549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08549)
- **Robnik & Seljak (2024)**, "Fluctuation without dissipation: Microcanonical Langevin Monte Carlo", arXiv:2303.18221, PMLR 253, 111–126 (2024) [jref]. Continuous-time MCLMC SDE. The stationary distribution is microcanonical with the correct configuration-space marginal, and the method is ergodic for any nonzero noise. — [arXiv:2303.18221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18221)
- **Robnik, Cohn-Gordon & Seljak (2024/2025)**, "Practical and Scalable Hamiltonian Monte Carlo Without the Metropolis Test", arXiv:2412.08876 (stat.CO; no venue on arXiv). Automatic step-size tuning for unadjusted HMC/LMC/MCLMC, set to meet a user-specified bias tolerance through an energy-error–bias relation (details in A4). On the ϕ⁴ lattice (>10⁶ dimensions): NUTS 133,266 gradients, adjusted MCLMC 39,240, unadjusted MCLMC 1,344. On U(1) gauge theory (about 2×10⁶ dimensions): unadjusted MCLMC 198 gradients versus 15,578–127,875 for adjusted methods. — [arXiv:2412.08876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08876)
- **Robnik, Cohn-Gordon & Seljak (2025)**, "Metropolis Adjusted Microcanonical Hamiltonian Monte Carlo" (MAMS), arXiv:2503.01707, NeurIPS 38 (2025) [Crossref record "Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 38", DOI 10.52202/085713-0513]. Adds an MH step to MCLMC dynamics, giving asymptotically unbiased samples with automated tuning. "Outperforms NUTS across the board ... up to a factor of seven speedup". The abstract notes that MCLMC "produces biased samples, with a bias that is hard to control in general". — [arXiv:2503.01707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01707)
- **Sommer, Robnik, Nozadze, Seljak & Rügamer (2025)**, "Microcanonical Langevin Ensembles: Advancing the Sampling of Bayesian Neural Networks", arXiv:2502.06335, ICLR 2025 [conference stated on the arXiv PDF header]. Ensembles of MCLMC chains; "speedups up to an order of magnitude" over NUTS-based approaches. Relevant as a recipe for many short chains. — [arXiv:2502.06335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06335)
- **Cabezas, Corenflos, Lao, Louf et al. (22 authors) (2024)**, "BlackJAX: Composable Bayesian inference in JAX", arXiv:2402.10797 (arXiv preprint; no journal found). Functional, composable JAX sampler library (HMC, NUTS, MCLMC, and others) that runs on CPU/GPU/TPU. — [arXiv:2402.10797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10797)
- **Modi, Barnett & Carpenter (2024)**, "Delayed rejection Hamiltonian Monte Carlo for sampling multiscale distributions", arXiv:2110.00610, Bayesian Analysis 19, 815 [Crossref, 10.1214/23-ba1360]. Retries with geometrically smaller steps after rejection; "up to five-fold performance gains over optimally-tuned HMC" (ESS per gradient). — [arXiv:2110.00610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00610)

### Inferences
- The project's half-step MCLMC at about 250 gradients/ESS is in the regime the Robnik/Seljak papers predict for unadjusted microcanonical dynamics. It is also about 4× better than the about $10^3$ model evaluations per ESS that Simon et al. 2025 report for cosmological parameters (A3), although the problems differ (PNG parameter vs Ωm/σ8; 2LPT vs PM).
- MAMS is the natural unbiased cross-check on the same dynamics. If MAMS reproduces the MCLMC $f_{\rm NL}$ posterior within Monte Carlo error at a few times the cost, that settles the bias question without NUTS.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the journal volume/pages for NUTS (JMLR 15) or MCHMC (JMLR 24) on a publisher page. Both are from memory.
- The BlackJAX paper has no journal publication that I could find.

## A3. Field-level samplers in cosmology: benchmarks, preconditioning, reparametrisation, flows

### Takeaway
The key benchmark is Simon, Lanusse & de Mattia (2025, JCAP), run on a JaxPM galaxy model with up to $128^3$ latents. Unadjusted MCLMC needs about $10^3$ model evaluations per effective sample for cosmology, which is about 10× better than adjusted samplers (HMC, NUTS, NUTS-within-Gibbs, MAMS). Preconditioning dominates: sampling in Fourier space with a static "Kaiser" (linear-theory posterior) scaling of the latent field gives most of the gain, while the adaptive diagonal mass matrix adds little.

### Cited Findings
**Benchmarks of samplers on field-level LSS**
- **Simon, Lanusse & de Mattia (2025)**, "Benchmarking field-level cosmological inference from galaxy redshift surveys", arXiv:2504.20130, JCAP 12 (2025) 039 [jref]. (The task brief calls the first author "Simon-Onfroy"; arXiv lists "Simon, Hugo". Code: github.com/hsimonfroy/benchmark-field-level.) Findings from the full text:
  - Baseline is $(320\,{\rm Mpc})^3$ on a $64^3$ mesh (5 Mpc voxels), so dim = 262,144. The resolution test goes to $128^3$.
  - Samplers: HMC, NUTS, NUTS-within-Gibbs, MAMS, MCLMC.
  - Best configuration (Table 2): $N_{\rm eval}/N_{\rm eff}$ = 1005 for cosmology. Ablations: 1136 without mass matrix, 1200 without dynamic Kaiser, 3112 without Kaiser, 6714 without Fourier-space sampling. For the linear field the cost is "below 10²" evaluations per effective sample.
  - MCLMC beats adjusted samplers by "approximately an order-of-magnitude".
  - Cost scaling for cosmology/bias: about $d^{1/5}$ when volume grows at fixed resolution, and about $d$ when resolution increases. The linear-field cost is almost independent of $d$ in volume scaling.
  - Integrators: McLachlan (2 grad/step) was best; KDK, Blanes and Omelyan were also tested. MCLMC decoherence length is $L=0.4\,(N_{\rm step}/\min_i N_{\rm eff})\,\varepsilon$, with thinning of 16.
  - At $128^3$ with a 5-step PM, a full posterior took under 4 h on one 8×80 GB GPU node, versus more than 80 h for NUTS.
  - Bias diagnosis is described in A4.
  - — [arXiv:2504.20130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20130); [full text v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20130v2)

### Inferences
- The white-noise parametrisation ($\varepsilon$ with unit prior) is fully non-centred. At high S/N modes (QSO $\bar nP\sim0.3$–0.5 at the peak, higher for LRG), a Kaiser/Wiener-type per-mode rescaling, which Bayer et al. write as $(P_L+N)/N$, should help further. The $f_{\rm NL}$ coupling enters through $\phi^2$, so a dynamic version that depends on $f_{\rm NL}$ and $b_\phi$ may matter more for PNG than for σ8.
- Simon et al. measure about $d^{1}$ cost scaling when resolution increases at fixed volume. Going from $80^3$ to $160^3$ (8× more dimensions at fixed volume) could therefore cost several times more gradients/ESS for bias-like parameters. The project's 250 gradients/ESS at $160^3$ is already the higher-resolution point.

## A4. Unadjusted-integrator bias (step-size dependence) and how to diagnose it

### Takeaway
For unadjusted HMC/LMC/MCLMC, asymptotic bias is controlled by the energy-error variance per dimension (EEVPD). For Gaussian targets there is a proven bound $b^2_{\rm cov}\le\varphi^{-1}({\rm EEVPD})$, and recommended targets are about $5\times10^{-4}$ (MCLMC, roughly 10% relative RMSE) down to about $3\times10^{-7}$ (1% RMSE). Field-level cosmology practice has used EEVPD of $10^{-4}$ (Bayer) and $10^{-6}$ (Simon et al.), checked by step-size halving or doubling against an adjusted sampler.

### Cited Findings
- **Robnik, Cohn-Gordon & Seljak (arXiv:2412.08876)** — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08876):
  - Theorem 4.2 (Gaussian targets): $b^2_{\rm cov}\le\varphi^{-1}({\rm EEVPD})$ with $\varphi(x)=4x^{3/2}/(1+x^{1/2})^2$, valid for EEVPD < 0.397. A Wasserstein-2 analogue holds for EEVPD < 6.75.
  - For a Gaussian, EEVPD = $(1/d)\sum_i E(\epsilon^2/\sigma_i^2)$ with $E(y)=y^3/[16(1-y/4)]$.
  - Recommended targets: $3\times10^{-4}$ for HMC/LMC (10% relative RMSE); $3\times10^{-7}$ for 1% RMSE; $5\times10^{-4}$ for MCLMC. Stochastic volatility needed $2\times10^{-8}$ for MCLMC.
  - The bound holds numerically on most non-Gaussian benchmarks. The exception is Brownian motion, where bias is about 1.5× the bound at small step size.
  - Bias metric: $b^2=(E_{\rm sampler}[x_i^2]-E[x_i^2])^2/{\rm Var}[x_i^2]$, averaged over parameters, with $b^2<0.01$ (about 100 effective samples) as the convergence threshold.
  - Suggested diagnostic: rerun at half the tuned step size and compare expectations.
- **Simon, Lanusse & de Mattia (2025)** set EEVPD = $10^{-6}$. In Appendix C, steps of 1ε, 1.5ε and 2ε (nominal ε = 33) gave EEVPD of $10^{-6}$, $4\times10^{-6}$ and $10^{-5}$, and the posteriors were compared with NUTS. They also check that $N_{\rm eval}\times{\rm MSE}[\mu]$ is the same order as $N_{\rm eval}/N_{\rm eff}$; a much larger MSE than $1/N_{\rm eff}$ would indicate bias. No detectable bias was found. — [arXiv:2504.20130v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20130v2)
- **Bayer, Seljak & Modi (2023)** tuned MCLMC so energy fluctuations per dimension are below $10^{-4}$ and validated against HMC at $32^3$ (qualitative agreement only). — [arXiv:2307.09504](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.09504)
- **Robnik, Cohn-Gordon & Seljak (2025, MAMS)**: MCLMC "produces biased samples, with a bias that is hard to control in general"; MAMS removes the bias with an MH step. — [arXiv:2503.01707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01707)

### Inferences
- For a Gaussian, $E(y)\propto y^3\propto\epsilon^6$ at small step. Halving the step should lower EEVPD by up to about 64× and lower the bias bound to match. Simon et al.'s nonlinear problem showed weaker scaling: EEVPD rose only about 10× from 1ε to 2ε. The project's "half the auto-tuned step" run is therefore the diagnostic Robnik et al. recommend. To complete it, report EEVPD at both steps and show that the $f_{\rm NL}$ mean shift between them is below $\sigma/\sqrt{N_{\rm eff}}$.
- The bias bound applies to covariance and second moments averaged over all $d$ parameters. With $d\sim4\times10^6$ field modes and one $f_{\rm NL}$, an average-over-parameters criterion can hide a bias in $f_{\rm NL}$. This argues for a per-parameter check on $f_{\rm NL}$ (MCLMC versus MAMS or NUTS on a reduced $80^3$ problem).

### Gaps
- I found no paper that quantifies unadjusted-sampler bias on $f_{\rm NL}$ or on any parameter coupled non-linearly to the field (e.g. through $\phi^2$).

## B1. Imaging-systematics mitigation and its impact on $f_{\rm NL}$

### Takeaway
Since BOSS, uncorrected imaging systematics have produced spurious large-scale power that mimics $f_{\rm NL}>0$. Mitigation by template regression (linear, then neural networks), mode projection or mode deprojection removes much of it but costs constraining power: in DESI photometric LRGs, nine maps "more than doubles" the $f_{\rm NL}$ uncertainty. Spectroscopic DESI DR1 QSOs show no excess large-scale power after linear weights, unlike photometric QSO targets.

## B2. Template sampling inside forward models (BORG foreground handling)

### Takeaway
BORG/ARES have two approaches. One block-samples coefficients of known foreground templates (Jasche & Lavaux 2017). The other uses a "robust likelihood" that marginalises unknown large-scale multiplicative contamination amplitudes per sky patch (Porqueres et al. 2019), which Lavaux et al. 2019 used for BOSS. A ready-made $f_{\rm NL}$-specific demonstration of template sampling at the field level does not exist yet.

### Cited Findings
- **Jasche & Lavaux (2017)**, "Bayesian power-spectrum inference with foreground and target contamination treatment", arXiv:1706.08971, A&A 606, A37 [jref]. Adds block sampling of the coefficients of a priori templates to ARES, jointly with density field, P(k), bias and noise. Demonstrated on mocks with stellar contamination and dust extinction. Template coefficients and P(k) amplitudes show "up to 10 percent correlations and anti-correlations across large ranges in Fourier space". — [arXiv:1706.08971](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08971)
- **Porqueres, Kodi Ramanah, Jasche & Lavaux (2019)**, "Explicit Bayesian treatment of unknown foreground contaminations in galaxy surveys", arXiv:1812.05113, A&A 624, A115 [jref]. A "robust likelihood" that marginalises unknown large-scale contamination amplitudes. On SDSS-III mocks with dust contamination, the Poisson likelihood "yields excessive power for large-scale modes" while the robust likelihood gives unbiased P(k) over all modes. — [arXiv:1812.05113](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05113)
- **Lavaux, Jasche & Leclercq (2019)**, "Systematic-free inference of the cosmic matter density field from SDSS3-BOSS data", arXiv:1909.06396 (no journal ref on arXiv; appears unpublished). BORG on BOSS with light cone, RSD, and known plus unknown foreground corrections. Shows no bias at $k\simeq10^{-3}$–$10^{-2}\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ in the posterior IC power spectrum, cross-correlates with Planck lensing, and produces maps of residual unexplained systematics. — [arXiv:1909.06396](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06396)
- **Andrews et al. (2026, Quaia)** chose conservative sky cuts for foreground robustness rather than template sampling. — [arXiv:2602.02363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02363)
- **Simon et al. (2025)** explicitly defer "survey-specific modeling choices (e.g. selection functions, observational systematics)" to future work (per search summary of the paper). — [arXiv:2504.20130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20130)
- **Rosado-Marín et al. (2025)** show that forward image simulations give a physically derived 3D selection function. This is an alternative or prior source for template shapes. — [arXiv:2411.12024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12024)

### Inferences
- The robust likelihood of Porqueres et al. is the field-level analogue of mode deprojection. It marginalises an amplitude per angular patch analytically, which costs large-scale information. Explicit template sampling (Jasche & Lavaux 2017) keeps more information if the templates are complete. For $f_{\rm NL}$, whose signal sits at $k\lesssim0.02$, this trade-off is central and should be quantified in Stage 4 with both approaches.
- Template amplitudes enter the expected counts linearly and multiplicatively, so they are cheap to include in joint NUTS/MCLMC. Their posterior correlation with $f_{\rm NL}$ (Jasche & Lavaux saw about 10% correlation with P(k)) could slow mixing; a per-template mass-matrix entry or analytic marginalisation may be needed.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that samples imaging-template amplitudes jointly with $f_{\rm NL}$ at the field level and reports the degradation in σ(f_NL). This is a novel element of Stage 4.

## B3. Integral constraints (AIC, RIC) and window treatment

### Takeaway
When the radial selection n(z) or the angular selection (imaging regression) is estimated from the data, power is removed on large scales: the radial integral constraint (RIC) and the angular integral constraint (AIC). Both act at exactly the scales that carry $f_{\rm NL}$, and ignoring them biases $f_{\rm NL}$ by order σ or more. DESI DR1 absorbs both into the window matrix.

### Cited Findings
- **de Mattia & Ruhlmann-Kleider (2019)**, "Integral constraints in spectroscopic surveys", arXiv:1904.08851, JCAP 08 (2019) 036 [jref]. Full derivation of the global IC and the RIC (from shuffled n(z)). RIC impact "can be as significant as the window function effect at large scales". Unknown angular systematics can be mitigated by nulling angular fluctuations, which is modelled as an AIC combinable with the RIC. — [arXiv:1904.08851](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08851)
- **de Mattia, Ruhlmann-Kleider, Raichoor, Ross, Tamone et al. (2021)**, "The Completed SDSS-IV eBOSS: BAO and growth rate of the ELG sample from the anisotropic power spectrum", arXiv:2007.09008, MNRAS 501, 5616 [jref]. First consistent application of the angular and radial integral constraints to data (ELG photometric selection variations). — [arXiv:2007.09008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09008)
- **Wilson, Peacock, Taylor & de la Torre (2017)**, "Rapid modelling of the redshift-space power spectrum multipoles for a masked density field", arXiv:1511.07799, MNRAS 464, 3121 [Crossref]. Window-convolved multipoles through Hankel transforms, including the integral-constraint formalism. — [arXiv:1511.07799](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.07799)
- **Riquelme, Avila, García-Bellido, Porredon, Ferrero et al. (2023)**, "PNG with Angular correlation function: Integral constraint and validation for DES", arXiv:2209.07187, MNRAS 523, 603 [Crossref]. Without the IC, GOLIAT-PNG mocks are biased by $\Delta f_{\rm NL}\sim100$. A DES-like forecast at $f_{\rm NL}=100$ gives $\Delta f_{\rm NL}\sim23$ (1.8σ); including the IC recovers truth within 1σ. — [arXiv:2209.07187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07187)
- **Chaussidon et al. (2025)**: "We improve the model of the radial integral constraint proposing an innovative correction of the window function", and mitigation is validated "by incorporating an angular integral constraint contribution to the window function". — [arXiv:2411.17623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17623)

### Inferences
- In a field-level forward model, the RIC disappears if n(z) is a model input with its own uncertainty rather than estimated from the data. The AIC disappears if imaging templates are sampled rather than regressed out beforehand. This is the motivation for the project's Stage 4 design. The shuffled-random RIC has no exact field-level analogue: at the field level one would sample n(z) shape parameters per shell.

## B4. Other PNG-specific systematics: relativistic/projection effects, magnification, redshift errors, stellar contamination

### Takeaway
GR projection effects and magnification add $k^{-2}$-like terms on near-horizon scales. For Newtonian-gauge or bias-definition mistakes the effect is equivalent to effective $f_{\rm NL}\lesssim0.5$–1. For QSOs it depends on the evolution bias $b_e$ and the magnification slope $s$, and it matters most at $k\sim10^{-3}$. These effects are subdominant at current DESI DR1 precision (σ ~ 9), but neglecting magnification has been forecast to bias $f_{\rm NL}$ by more than 1σ for future spectroscopic surveys. Stellar contamination is the main QSO-specific contaminant in imaging-based samples and is reduced to a few percent in DESI spectroscopy.

### Cited Findings
**GR / projection effects**
- **Yoo, Fitzpatrick & Zaldarriaga (2009)**, "New Perspective on Galaxy Clustering as a Cosmological Probe: General Relativistic Effects", arXiv:0907.0707, PRD 80, 083514 [jref]. — [arXiv:0907.0707](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.0707)
- **Yoo (2010)**, "General Relativistic Description of the Observed Galaxy Power Spectrum: Do We Understand What We Measure?", arXiv:1009.3021, PRD 82, 083508 [jref]. Large-scale GR corrections "can be misinterpreted as the detection of the primordial non-Gaussianity". — [arXiv:1009.3021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.3021)
- **Challinor & Lewis (2011)**, "The linear power spectrum of observed source number counts", arXiv:1105.5292, PRD 84, 043516 [jref]. Full linear GR number counts, including magnification and evolution terms. — [arXiv:1105.5292](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.5292)
- **Bonvin & Durrer (2011)**, "What galaxy surveys really measure", arXiv:1105.5280, PRD 84, 063505 [jref]. — [arXiv:1105.5280](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.5280)
- **Bruni, Crittenden, Koyama, Maartens, Pitrou & Wands (2012)**, "Disentangling non-Gaussianity, bias and GR effects in the galaxy distribution", arXiv:1106.3999, PRD 85, 041301 [jref]. GR corrections and gauge ambiguity in the bias definition mimic $f_{\rm NL}={\cal O}(1)$ but are distinguishable by redshift and scale dependence. — [arXiv:1106.3999](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.3999)
- **Jeong, Schmidt & Hirata (2012)**, "Large-scale clustering of galaxies in general relativity", arXiv:1107.5427, PRD 85, 023504 [jref]. Linear bias is gauge-consistent in synchronous-comoving gauge. The residual GR corrections act like an effective local $f_{\rm NL}<0.5$. — [arXiv:1107.5427](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.5427)
- **Raccanelli, Bertacca, Doré & Maartens (2014)**, "Large-scale 3D galaxy correlation function and non-Gaussianity", arXiv:1306.6646, JCAP 08 (2014) 022 [jref]. Wide-angle, Doppler and potential terms are comparable in magnitude to small-$f_{\rm NL}$ corrections in the multipoles. — [arXiv:1306.6646](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.6646)
- **Camera, Santos & Maartens (2015)**, "Probing PNG with SKA galaxy redshift surveys: a fully relativistic analysis", arXiv:1409.8286, MNRAS 448, 1035 (erratum MNRAS 467, 1505) [jref]. — [arXiv:1409.8286](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.8286)
- **Wang, Beutler & Bacon (2020)**, "Impact of Relativistic Effects on the PNG Signature in the Large-Scale Clustering of Quasars", arXiv:2007.01802, MNRAS 499, 2598 [Crossref]. $b_e$ and $s$ are derived from eBOSS QSO luminosity functions. For DESI, relativistic corrections "can overwhelm the expected $f_{\rm NL}$ signature at low redshifts $z\lesssim1$ and become comparable to $f_{\rm NL}\simeq1$ in the power spectrum quadrupole at $z\gtrsim2.5$", at $k\sim10^{-3}\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$. The authors recommend forward modelling from the luminosity function. Note that "overwhelm" refers to the expected signal for small $f_{\rm NL}$, not to DR1 error bars. — [arXiv:2007.01802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01802)
- **Castorina & Di Dio (2022)**, "The observed galaxy power spectrum in General Relativity", arXiv:2106.08857, JCAP 01 (2022) 061 [memory, unverified]. IR divergences cancel once all GR terms are included. GR effects including magnification are "a small, less than ten percent level, correction" to the leading RSD term in the multipoles. — [arXiv:2106.08857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08857)
- **Foglieni, Pantiri, Di Dio & Castorina (2023)**, "Large Scale Limit of the Observed Galaxy Power Spectrum", arXiv:2303.03142, PRL 131, 111201 [memory, unverified]. Relativistic effects "could easily account for 10% of the observed power spectrum", but the subset with PNG-like scale dependence "can be safely ignored for current galaxy surveys". — [arXiv:2303.03142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03142)

**Magnification**
- **Namikawa, Okamura & Taruya (2011)**, "Magnification effect on the detection of PNG from photometric surveys", arXiv:1103.1118, PRD 83, 123514 [jref]. Ignoring magnification biases $f_{\rm NL}$ beyond 3σ for deep photometric surveys. Adding shear reduces the bias, but it remains serious for $z_m>1.5$ (LSST). — [arXiv:1103.1118](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.1118)
- **Lorenz, Alonso & Ferreira (2018)**, "The impact of relativistic effects on cosmological parameter estimation", arXiv:1710.02477, PRD 97, 023537 [jref]. Per a search summary of the full text, magnification alone can bias $f_{\rm NL}$ by about 1σ without CMB or shear data, through mis-estimated bias. — [arXiv:1710.02477](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02477)
- **Viljoen, Fonseca & Maartens (2021)**, "Multi-wavelength spectroscopic probes: biases from neglecting light-cone effects", arXiv:2108.05746, JCAP 12 (2021) 004 [jref]. For DESI, Euclid and SKAO: neglecting lensing magnification produces "a bias of more than 1σ" on $f_{\rm NL}$ and on standard parameters. — [arXiv:2108.05746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05746)

**Redshift errors (QSO)**
- **Smith, Burtin, Hou, Neveux, Ross et al. (2020)**, "The Completed SDSS-IV eBOSS: N-body Mock Challenge for the Quasar Sample", arXiv:2007.09003, MNRAS 499, 269 [Crossref]. Tests redshift smearing and catastrophic redshifts on QSO RSD/BAO fits; reports systematics of $\sigma_{f\sigma_8}=0.013$. It does not test $f_{\rm NL}$. — [arXiv:2007.09003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09003)
- **Chaussidon et al. (2023)** report >99% purity for the automated DESI QSO catalogue. — [arXiv:2208.08511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08511)

**Stellar contamination**
- 2.6% stellar contamination in DESI QSO targets (Krolewski et al. 2024) — [arXiv:2305.07650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07650)
- Residual stellar contamination in imaging-selected QSO targets (Chaussidon et al. 2022) — [arXiv:2108.03640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03640)
- A Gaia stellar template best for eBOSS QSO (Rezaie et al. 2021) — [arXiv:2106.13724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13724)
- SFB evidence of residual stellar contamination in eBOSS QSO at $p\lesssim0.002$ (Bruton et al. 2026) — [arXiv:2605.10935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10935)
- Stellar density and stellar colour locus as major photometric-QSO contaminants (Pullen & Hirata 2013) — [arXiv:1212.4500](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.4500)
- Spectroscopic DR1 QSOs show no excess large-scale autopower (Chiarenza et al. 2026) — [arXiv:2512.17865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17865)

### Inferences
- For DR1 (σ ~ 9) and the Stage 1 go/no-go target (σ ≲ 5), the GR projection terms are at or below 1 in effective $f_{\rm NL}$ (Jeong et al. <0.5; Wang et al. ~1 at z ≳ 2.5 in the quadrupole). They can be ignored for Stage 1–3 but should enter the Stage 4 systematics budget. Magnification is the most likely to matter (>1σ in Viljoen et al. forecasts for DESI) and needs $s(z)$ for DESI QSOs. In a field-level model the lowest-order magnification term ($\propto(5s-2)\kappa$) is a line-of-sight integral of the density field, which is computable from the forward model.
- The QSO redshift-error literature I found addresses RSD/BAO only. A catastrophic-redshift fraction acts as a mixture that moves objects between shells. That suppresses inter-shell decorrelation and could mimic or dilute large-scale power, and it should be modelled as a shell-mixing matrix in the selection function.

### Gaps
- I found no paper quantifying the impact of DESI QSO catastrophic redshift failures on $f_{\rm NL}$.
- I did not verify the journal references for Castorina & Di Dio (JCAP 01 (2022) 061) or Foglieni et al. (PRL 131, 111201); both are from memory.
- I found no DESI-specific measurement of the QSO magnification slope $s(z)$ for DR1 in this search. Wang et al. 2020 use eBOSS luminosity functions.
