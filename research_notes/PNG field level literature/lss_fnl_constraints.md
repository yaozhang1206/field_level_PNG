# Observational constraints and forecasts on local $f_{\rm NL}$ from large-scale structure (bibliography, as of 2026-10-13)

*How the entries were checked: every arXiv ID below was confirmed against its arxiv.org/abs page. The page gave the title, authors, date and abstract. Journal references come from the INSPIRE-HEP API. "J: not listed" means neither arXiv nor INSPIRE gave a journal reference on 2026-10-13. ## Q1. Theory foundations of scale-dependent bias

### Takeaway
Three groups derived the $\Delta b \propto f_{\rm NL}/k^2$ scale-dependent bias in 2008: Dalal et al. with N-body simulations, Matarrese & Verde with peak statistics, and Slosar et al. with the peak-background split. Slosar et al. also showed that it rests on mass-function universality and is suppressed for recent mergers, which is the origin of $p=1.6$. Later work, including separate-universe simulations, TNG and assembly bias studies, showed that $b_\phi$ is the dominant theoretical systematic. A wrong $b_\phi(b_1)$ relation can change $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})$ by up to an order of magnitude.

### Cited Findings
**Original derivations**
- **Dalal, Doré, Huterer & Shirokov 2008**, "The imprints of primordial non-gaussianities on large-scale structure: scale dependent bias and abundance of virialized objects", arXiv:0710.4560, PRD 77, 123514. N-body simulations plus analytic arguments find strongly scale-dependent halo bias. A rough estimate says upcoming surveys could reach $|f_{\rm NL}| \lesssim 10$. — [arXiv:0710.4560](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.4560)
- **Matarrese & Verde 2008**, "The effect of primordial non-Gaussianity on halo bias", arXiv:0801.4826, ApJL 677, L77. Analytic halo bias as a function of scale, mass and redshift for non-Gaussian models, using the high-peak and large-separation approximations. — [arXiv:0801.4826](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.4826)
- **Slosar, Hirata, Seljak, Ho & Padmanabhan 2008**, "Constraints on local primordial non-Gaussianity from large scale structure", arXiv:0805.3580, JCAP 08 (2008) 031.
  - Rederives the effect with the peak-background split and shows it depends only on mass-function universality.
  - Extended Press-Schechter suggests the effect is suppressed for recent mergers, which is the origin of the QSO $p=1.6$ convention.
  - Data: SDSS photometric QSOs, LRGs and NVSS, among others.
  - Result: $-29 < f_{\rm NL} < 70$ (95%), or $-31 < f_{\rm NL} < 70$ if QSOs are recent mergers.
  - — [arXiv:0805.3580](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.3580)
- **Afshordi & Tolley 2008**, "Primordial non-gaussianity, statistics of collapsed objects, and the Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect", arXiv:0806.1046, PRD 78, 123507.
  - Confirms the non-local bias with a modified-Gaussian initial-conditions argument.
  - NVSS×CMB (ISW) hint: $f_{\rm NL}=236\pm127$.
  - Forecasts $\Delta f_{\rm NL}\sim0.1$ (auto) and $\sim3$ (×CMB).
  - — [arXiv:0806.1046](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.1046)
- **Desjacques, Seljak & Iliev 2009**, "Scale-dependent bias induced by local non-Gaussianity: A comparison to N-body simulations", arXiv:0811.2748, MNRAS 396, 85.
  - Theory and simulations agree for $k<0.03\,h$/Mpc when $b>1.5$.
  - For $b<1.5$–2 the simulated effect exceeds the prediction.
  - — [arXiv:0811.2748](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.2748)

**Second-order and non-local bias**
- **Baldauf, Seljak & Senatore 2011**, "Primordial non-Gaussianity in the Bispectrum of the Halo Density Field", arXiv:1011.1513, JCAP 04 (2011) 006.
  - Tree-level halo bispectrum with non-Gaussian bias terms; the $b_{\phi\delta}$-type amplifications appear here.
  - Finds that the bispectrum $f_{\rm NL}$ signal-to-noise can greatly exceed the power spectrum's.
  - — [arXiv:1011.1513](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.1513)
- **Scoccimarro, Hui, Manera & Chan 2012**, "Large-scale Bias and Efficient Generation of Initial Conditions for Non-Local Primordial Non-Gaussianity", arXiv:1108.5512, PRD 85, 083002.
  - General peak-background-split bias formula, including non-universal mass functions.
  - Gives a recipe for generating non-local PNG initial conditions.
  - — [arXiv:1108.5512](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.5512)

**Reviews**
- **Desjacques & Seljak 2010**, "Primordial non-Gaussianity from the large scale structure", arXiv:1003.5020, CQG 27, 124011. Review of LSS searches for PNG. — [arXiv:1003.5020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.5020)
- **Desjacques, Jeong & Schmidt 2018**, "Large-Scale Galaxy Bias", arXiv:1611.09787, Phys. Rept. 733, 1. The standard bias review, with a dedicated treatment of PNG bias ($b_\phi$, $b_{\phi\delta}$). — [arXiv:1611.09787](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09787)
- **Biagetti 2019**, "The Hunt for Primordial Interactions in the Large Scale Structures of the Universe", arXiv:1906.12244, Galaxies 7, 71. Review connecting inflationary correlators to late-time halo statistics. — [arXiv:1906.12244](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.12244)
- **Meerburg et al. 2019**, "Primordial Non-Gaussianity" (Astro2020 white paper), arXiv:1903.04409, BAAS 51, 107. — [arXiv:1903.04409](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04409)
- **Achúcarro et al. 2022**, "Inflation: Theory and Observations" (Snowmass white paper), arXiv:2203.08128, J: not listed. Surveys the observational targets for PNG, including the $\sigma(f_{\rm NL}^{\rm loc})\sim1$ single-field vs multi-field threshold. — [arXiv:2203.08128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08128)

**$b_\phi$ / assembly bias (the key theory systematic)**
- **Biagetti et al. 2017**, "Verifying the consistency relation for the scale-dependent bias from local primordial non-Gaussianity", arXiv:1611.04901, MNRAS 468, 3277.
  - The non-Gaussian bias matches the response of the mass function to the primordial amplitude.
  - The universal-mass-function prediction overpredicts it by 10–15% for high-mass halos.
  - — [arXiv:1611.04901](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04901)
- **Barreira et al. 2020**, "Galaxy bias and primordial non-Gaussianity: insights from galaxy formation simulations with IllustrisTNG", arXiv:2006.09368, JCAP 12 (2020) 013.
  - For stellar-mass-selected galaxies, $b_\phi = 2\delta_c(b_1-p)$ with $p\in[0.4,0.7]$, not $p=1$.
  - This is the origin of Andrews et al.'s $p=0.55$.
  - — [arXiv:2006.09368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09368)
- **Barreira 2020**, "On the impact of galaxy bias uncertainties on primordial non-Gaussianity constraints", arXiv:2009.06622, JCAP 12 (2020) 031.
  - The $f_{\rm NL}$–$p$ plane is bimodal.
  - Fixing $p=1$ when the truth is $p=0.55$ biases $f_{\rm NL}$ by 0.5–1$\sigma$.
  - A prior of $\Delta p\approx0.5$ is sufficient.
  - — [arXiv:2009.06622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06622)
- **Barreira 2022**, "Can we actually constrain $f_{\rm NL}$ using the scale-dependent bias effect?", arXiv:2205.05673, JCAP 11 (2022) 013.
  - BOSS DR12 $P(k)$: $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})$ varies by about 10× depending on the assumed $b_\phi(b_1)$, from $16\pm16$ to $230\pm226$.
  - Marginalizing with wide $b_\phi$ priors gives biased constraints through projection effects.
  - — [arXiv:2205.05673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05673)
- **Lazeyras, Barreira, Schmidt & Desjacques 2023**, "Assembly bias in the local PNG halo bias and its implication for $f_{\rm NL}$ constraints", arXiv:2209.07251, JCAP 01 (2023) 023.
  - Concentration drives $b_1$ and $b_\phi$ in opposite directions. At $z=0.5$, $b_1\approx2$: $b_\phi\approx3$ (all halos), $-1$ (low concentration), $9$ (high concentration).
  - BOSS $\sigma_{f_{\rm NL}} = 44, 165, 19$ for the three cases.
  - — [arXiv:2209.07251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07251)
- **Fondi, Verde, Villaescusa-Navarro, Baldi et al. 2024**, "Taming assembly bias for primordial non-Gaussianity", arXiv:2311.10088, JCAP 02 (2024) 048. A photometric proxy for assembly bias, from hydrodynamical simulations, gives a $p$ prior that degrades $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})$ only mildly. — [arXiv:2311.10088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10088)
- **Hadzhiyska, Garrison, Eisenstein & Ferraro 2024**, "AbacusPNG: A modest set of simulations of local-type primordial non-Gaussianity in the DESI era", arXiv:2402.10881, PRD 109, 103530.
  - Nine $2\,h^{-1}$Gpc boxes.
  - Strong $b_\phi$ response to concentration, shear and accretion rate.
  - The galaxy-halo model uncertainty on $b_\phi$ alone gives $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})$ of 21, 6 and 22 for LRG $z=0.5$, LRG $z=0.8$ and QSO $z=1.4$.
  - — [arXiv:2402.10881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10881)

### Inferences
- The 40% $b_\phi$ prior in the project brief is about as wide as the spread found in TNG and AbacusPNG. Barreira 2022 warns that wide $b_\phi$ priors cause projection-effect biases in posterior means. The Stage 2 MCMC should check this, because a Fisher analysis cannot show it.

### Gaps
- I did not separately verify the Desjacques & Seljak 2010 "Advances in Astronomy" review (a second DS review exists). Only arXiv:1003.5020 (CQG) is verified here.

## Q2. Spectroscopic analyses (SDSS/BOSS/eBOSS, DESI)

### Takeaway
LSS precision on $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm loc}$ has improved from $\sigma\sim50$ (BOSS DR9) to $\sigma\approx21$ (eBOSS DR16). With DESI DR1 it is now $\sigma\approx7$–9.
- $P(k)$ alone: $-3.6^{+9.0}_{-9.1}$.
- With a TNG-calibrated $p$ prior: $-3.3\pm9.2$.
- With LRG×QSO cross-correlations: $2.1^{+8.8}_{-8.3}$.
- With the EFT one-loop $P$+$B$: $-0.1\pm7.4$.

I found no DESI DR2/Y3 $f_{\rm NL}$ paper as of 2026-10-13.

### Inferences
- **Action for the Stage 1 Part A configuration.** The brief's LRG numbers are 2,130,621 objects with $0.4<z<1.1$. To reproduce Chaussidon's LRG $\sigma$, Part A should use $0.6<z<1.1$ with 1,631,716 LRGs. For Part B the 0.4–0.6 shell is a forecast choice, but it is not in the DESI baseline.
- The best spectroscopic LSS result to date is $\sigma\approx7.4$ (Chudaykin et al.). It comes from full-shape EFT $P$+$B$, so it already uses some of the non-Gaussian information that the field-level method targets. A field-level DR1 result should be compared with both Chaussidon (about 9) and Chudaykin (about 7.4), not only with Chaussidon.
- The $p$ assumption for QSOs moves the central value by about 7 ($-3.6$ vs $+3.5$; Chaussidon et al.). The TNG $p$ prior of Fondi et al. ($\bar p\simeq1.4$) is between the two conventions.

### Gaps
- No DESI DR2, Y3 or "DESI 2025" $f_{\rm NL}$ paper turned up in INSPIRE or arXiv searches for "non-Gaussianity DESI" and "DESI DR2 non-Gaussianity" on 2026-10-13. One may exist under a different title.
- The Chudaykin et al. abstract does not state the $b_\phi$/$p$ treatment behind $\sigma=7.4$. Check this in the paper before comparing with Chaussidon.
- An earlier arXiv version of D'Amico et al. 2201.11518 may have quoted different local-$f_{\rm NL}$ numbers. Only the current abstract was read.

## Q3. Photometric, radio and quasar-catalogue analyses (angular $C_\ell$)

### Takeaway
Angular analyses went from loose or spurious NVSS-era results to $\sigma\approx20$ (SDSS photometric QSOs, 2014). The best projected-statistics result is now Quaia with angular redshift fluctuations and CMB lensing, $f_{\rm NL}=-3\pm14$. Imaging systematics are the limiting factor throughout. In the DESI photometric LRGs, regressing 9 maps more than doubles $\sigma$.

## Q4. CMB-lensing cross-correlations, multi-tracer, and other cross-correlation constraints

### Takeaway
Cross-correlation with CMB lensing reduces sensitivity to additive imaging systematics. Measured errors are still $\sigma\sim20$–40:
- DESI QSO targets ×Planck: $-26^{+45}_{-40}$.
- DESI LRG ×Planck: $24^{+20}_{-21}$ with the auto-correlation.
- DESI DR1 spectroscopic QSO ×PR4: $2^{+28}_{-34}$.
- CIB ×lensing: $43\pm23$.

Multi-tracer sample-variance cancellation (Seljak 2009) is the theoretical route to $\sigma\lesssim1$.

### Cited Findings
**Multi-tracer theory and forecasts**
- **Seljak 2009**, "Measuring primordial non-gaussianity without cosmic variance", arXiv:0807.1770, PRL 102, 021302.
  - Correlating biased and unbiased tracers cancels cosmic variance.
  - The squared error improves as $\bar nP/2$, up to about 7× for an ideal survey to $z=2$.
  - — [arXiv:0807.1770](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.1770)
- **McDonald & Seljak 2009**, "How to measure redshift-space distortions without sample variance", arXiv:0810.0323, JCAP 10 (2009) 007. Extends the multi-tracer method, including to non-Gaussianity. — [arXiv:0810.0323](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.0323)
- **Ferraro & Smith 2015**, "Using Large Scale Structure to test Multifield Inflation", arXiv:1408.3126, PRD 91, 043506.
  - Survey with $V=25\,h^{-3}$Gpc³, $z=0.7$, $b=2.5$: $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})=6$.
  - With mass-weighted halos down to $10^{11}\,h^{-1}M_\odot$: $\sigma=1.5$.
  - — [arXiv:1408.3126](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.3126)
- **Ferramacho, Santos, Jarvis & Camera 2014**, "Radio Galaxy populations and the multi-tracer technique", arXiv:1402.2290, MNRAS 442, 2511. Multi-tracer radio continuum: $\sigma=3.6$ at 10 µJy, 2.2 at 1 µJy; the undifferentiated sample gives 48 and 12. — [arXiv:1402.2290](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.2290)
- **Alonso & Ferreira 2015**, "Constraining ultra large-scale cosmology with multiple tracers in optical and radio surveys", arXiv:1507.03550, PRD 92, 063525. Multi-tracer forecasts for GR effects and $f_{\rm NL}$ with DES, LSST and SKA. — [arXiv:1507.03550](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.03550)
- **Sullivan, Prijon & Seljak 2023**, "Learning to Concentrate: Multi-tracer Forecasts on Local Primordial Non-Gaussianity with Machine-Learned Bias", arXiv:2303.08901, JCAP 08 (2023) 004.
  - Splitting DESI-like samples by an ML-predicted $b_\phi$ gives $\sigma=2.3$ (ELG), 3.7 (LRG) and 1.5 (ELG+LRG overlap).

  - — [arXiv:2303.08901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08901)

**CMB-lensing cross-correlation**
- **Giannantonio & Percival 2014**, "Using correlations between CMB lensing and large-scale structure to measure primordial non-Gaussianity", arXiv:1312.5154, MNRAS 441, L16.   - — [arXiv:2412.10279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10279)

LBG × Planck lensing forecasts give $\sigma=20$–34, and 20 with DESI-II follow-up. — [arXiv:2511.22243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22243)

**kSZ**

- See Rosado-Marín et al. 2026 (Q2): DESI DR1 LRG×QSO gives $2.1^{+8.8}_{-8.3}$. — [arXiv:2604.05213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05213)

### Inferences
- In the DESI DR1 QSO footprint, CMB-lensing cross-correlation is about 3× weaker than the 3D auto-power: $\sigma\sim30$ versus about 10. Adding $\kappa$ to the field-level likelihood is therefore a robustness check, not a precision lever, at DR1. This matters for the brief's "if $\gtrsim7$, add CMB-lensing" fallback.

### Gaps
- None of the cross-correlation papers above reports a combined auto+cross DESI DR1 spectroscopic 3D + $\kappa$ result.

## Q5. Bispectrum / $P$+$B$ constraints and Quijote-PNG summary-statistic studies

### Takeaway
Adding the bispectrum to $P$ helps local $f_{\rm NL}$ by about 15–20% on current data (BOSS: Cabass et al.; eBOSS QSO: Cagliari et al. 2025). The EFT one-loop $P$+$B$ analysis of DESI DR1 gives the best LSS result, $\sigma=7.4$. In Quijote-PNG, local-PNG information in halos is dominated by large-scale scale-dependent bias and saturates by $k\sim0.2$. BORG field-level inference gains about 1.3× over $P$+$B$ at $k_{\max}\approx0.1$.

### Cited Findings
**Data**
- Cabass et al. 2022 (BOSS $P$+$B$, $-33\pm28$), D'Amico et al. (BOSS EFT $P$+$B$, $52\pm34$), Cagliari et al. 2025 (eBOSS QSO $P$+$B$, $-6<f_{\rm NL}<20$) and Chudaykin et al. 2025 (DESI DR1 $P$+$B$, $-0.1\pm7.4$) are listed under Q2. — [2204.01781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01781); [2201.11518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11518); [2502.14758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14758); [2512.04266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04266)

**Simulation and likelihood validation**
- **Moradinezhad Dizgah, Biagetti, Sefusatti, Desjacques & Noreña 2021**, "Primordial Non-Gaussianity from Biased Tracers: Likelihood Analysis of Real-Space Power Spectrum and Bispectrum", arXiv:2010.14523, JCAP 05 (2021) 015.
  - Halo $P$ (one-loop) plus $B$ (tree) improves $f_{\rm NL}$ by more than 5× over $P$ alone with loose priors.
  - Imposing UMF $b_\phi$ and $b_{\phi\delta}$ relations shifts $f_{\rm NL}$ systematically.
  - A Poisson-noise assumption also biases the result.
  - — [arXiv:2010.14523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14523)
- **Euclid Collaboration: Linde, Moradinezhad Dizgah, Parimbelli, Pardede et al. 2026**, "Euclid preparation: Testing multi-field inflation with galaxy power spectrum and bispectrum", arXiv:2605.21436, J: not listed.
  - AbacusPNG HOD mocks, one-loop $P_\ell$ plus tree $B_\ell$.
  - $B_\ell$ alone reduces $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})$ by 29–46% versus $P_\ell$; the joint fit gains another 8–13%.
  - Prior-agnostic detection of $f_{\rm NL}b_\phi$ is hard in single $z$-bins.
  - Proposes a physically motivated $b_\phi$ prior.
  - — [arXiv:2605.21436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21436)

**Quijote-PNG series**
- **Coulton, Villaescusa-Navarro, Jamieson, Baldi et al. 2023a**, "Quijote-PNG: Simulations of primordial non-Gaussianity and the information content of the matter field power spectrum and bispectrum", arXiv:2206.01619, ApJ 943, 64. Matter field, $k_{\max}=0.5$: $\Delta f_{\rm NL}^{\rm loc}\sim16$ per $1\,(h^{-1}{\rm Gpc})^3$ at $z=1$. — [arXiv:2206.01624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01624)

**Field-level**
- **Andrews, Jasche, Lavaux & Schmidt 2023**, "Bayesian field-level inference of primordial non-Gaussianity using next-generation galaxy surveys", arXiv:2203.08838, MNRAS 520, 5746. - **Andrews, Jasche, Lavaux, Coulton et al. 2026**, "Field-Level Inference of Primordial Non-Gaussianity with the Quijote Simulation Suite", arXiv:2603.20855, J: not listed. First BORG $f_{\rm NL}$ inference on N-body halo catalogues: about 1.3× better than $P$+$B$ at $k_{\max}\approx0.1$. — [arXiv:2603.20855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20855)
- **Chen, Green & Lee 2026**, "Primordial Non-Gaussianity and the Field-Level Cramer-Rao Bound", arXiv:2603.22415, J: not listed.
  - Field-level Cramér-Rao bound analysis.
  - Multi-tracer scale-dependent bias can beat conservative higher-point analyses for local PNG.

### Inferences
- The gain from adding the bispectrum on real data (about 16–20%; Cabass, Cagliari) is much smaller than idealized forecasts (more than 5×; Moradinezhad Dizgah et al.). This gap is likely driven by $b_\phi$/$b_{\phi\delta}$ freedom and stochasticity.
- Expect a similar gap between the Stage 1 field-level Fisher and what Stage 3 recovers on halos. Andrews et al. 2026 find only 1.3× over $P$+$B$.

### Gaps
- I did not check whether Chudaykin et al.'s $\sigma=7.4$ benefits mostly from $P$ (scale-dependent bias) or from $B$.

## Q6. Forecasts (DESI, Euclid, SPHEREx, LSST, MegaMapper, multi-tracer; the $\sigma\sim1$ target)

### Takeaway
The community target is $\sigma(f_{\rm NL}^{\rm loc})\lesssim1$, which separates single-field from multi-field inflation. Forecasts that reach it rely on all-sky volume plus multi-tracer and/or bispectrum, and on knowing $b_\phi$:
- SPHEREx: $\sigma\approx0.5$ goal; 0.7 from the bispectrum alone.
- kSZ + LSST: about 0.5.
- An ideal $b_\phi$-split multi-tracer analysis: below 1.

DESI Y5 $P(k)$ is forecast at about 6.5 (Chaussidon mocks), and Euclid field-level at about 2.3.

### Cited Findings
- **Planck 2018 IX (Akrami et al. 2020)**, arXiv:1905.05697, A&A 641, A9. CMB reference: $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm loc}=-0.9\pm5.1$. — [arXiv:1905.05697](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05697)
- **Giannantonio, Porciani, Carron, Amara & Pillepich 2012**, "Constraining primordial non-Gaussianity with future galaxy surveys", arXiv:1109.0958, MNRAS 422, 2854. Euclid-like clustering plus weak lensing two-point Fisher forecast: $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})\sim3$. — [arXiv:1109.0958](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.0958)

### Inferences
- The project's go/no-go threshold, field-level DR1 $\sigma\lesssim5$, would put DESI DR1 at Planck precision ($5.1$). It would be better than the Y5 $P(k)$ forecast (about 6.5) and the current best DR1 analysis (7.4).
